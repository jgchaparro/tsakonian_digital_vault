The Tsakonian verbal system blends conservative Doric features with unique innovations. Its two defining traits are a productive system of **grammatical aspect** — whether an action is viewed as ongoing or repeated (imperfective) versus completed or single (perfective) — and the heavy use of **periphrastic** (compound) constructions where Standard Modern Greek (SMG) uses a single inflected word. For example, 'I see' is not one word but the auxiliary _ένι_ ('I am') plus a participle:

<div style="text-align: center">

_Ένι ορού._<br>
*Éni oroú.*<br>
'I see.' / 'I am seeing.'

</div>

Tsakonian is in fact the only Greek dialect whose present and imperfect are **exclusively** periphrastic, a defining feature that sets it apart from every other Greek variety [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].

> **Grammar glossary:**
>
> - **Aspect**: whether an action is viewed as ongoing/repeated (imperfective) or completed/single (perfective) — a distinction more prominent in Tsakonian than in English.
> - **Voice**: whether the subject does the action (active) or receives it (passive).
> - **Periphrastic**: a construction using two words (an auxiliary + a main-verb form) instead of a single inflected form, e.g. _ένι ορού_ (*éni oroú*, 'I see').
> - **Participle**: a verb form used like an adjective or as part of a compound tense (English 'seeing', 'seen').
>
> See [[1.3 Doric heritage]] for the historical origin of the periphrastic system.

## Mood and Tense

Tsakonian distinguishes several **moods** — indicative, subjunctive, imperative, and conditional — and within each a set of **tenses** marking time and aspect. The system corresponds closely to that of Standard Modern Greek, with one major exception: Tsakonian preserves a morphologically distinct **continuous (imperfective) present subjunctive**, e.g. _να ορίνου_ (*na orínou*, 'to keep seeing'), which no longer exists in SMG, where only the perfective _να οράου_ (*na oraoú*, 'to see [once]') survives [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].

### Indicative mood

The **indicative** mood is used to make factual statements and describe reality directly, as in _ένι γράφου ένα γράμμα_ (*éni gráfou éna grámma*, 'I am writing a letter').

#### Present

Expresses **ongoing** or **habitual** actions at the time of speaking. It is always formed periphrastically (auxiliary _ένι_ + present participle), unlike Standard Modern Greek.

<div style="text-align: center">

_Ένι ορού._<br>
*Éni oroú.*<br>
'I see.' / 'I am seeing.'

</div>

#### Imperfect

Expresses **ongoing** or **habitual** actions in the past. Also periphrastic (auxiliary _έμα_ + present participle).

<div style="text-align: center">

_Έμα ορού._<br>
*Éma oroú.*<br>
'I was seeing.' / 'I used to see.'

</div>

#### Aorist (simple past)

Expresses a **single, completed** past action. Unlike the present and imperfect, the aorist is formed synthetically (a single word).

<div style="text-align: center">

_Οράκα._<br>
*Oráka.*<br>
'I saw.'

</div>

#### Perfect

Expresses a **past action with present relevance** — a completed event whose results still bear on the present. Formed with _ένι έχου_ ('I have') + perfect participle (the verbal adjective in _-τέ_).

<div style="text-align: center">

_Ένι έχου φτατέ._<br>
*Éni échou ftaté.*<br>
'I have arrived.'

</div>

#### Pluperfect

Expresses a **past action completed before another past event**. Formed with _έμα έχου_ ('I had') + perfect participle.

<div style="text-align: center">

_Έμα έχου φτατέ._<br>
*Éma échou ftaté.*<br>
'I had arrived.'

</div>

#### Future continuous

Expresses **ongoing** or **habitual** actions in the future. Formed with _θα_ + imperfective (continuous) present subjunctive.

<div style="text-align: center">

_Θα ορίνου._<br>
*Za orínou.*<br>
'I will be seeing.' / 'I will keep seeing.'

</div>

#### Future simple

Expresses a **single, completed** action in the future. Formed with _θα_ + perfective (aorist) subjunctive.

<div style="text-align: center">

_Θα οράου._<br>
*Za oraoú.*<br>
'I will see.'

</div>

#### Future perfect

Expresses an action that **will be completed before a future point** in time. Formed with _θα έχου_ + perfect participle.

<div style="text-align: center">

_Θα έχου φτατέ._<br>
*Za échou ftaté.*<br>
'I will have arrived.'

</div>

### Subjunctive mood

The **subjunctive** is introduced by the particle _να_ and expresses **wishes, intentions, purpose, obligation, and possibility**, as in _ένι θέου να οράου_ (*éni zéou na oraoú*, 'I want to see'). It also serves as the base for the future and conditional forms. Unlike Standard Modern Greek — where only the perfective subjunctive survives — Tsakonian maintains a morphological distinction between a **continuous (imperfective)** and a **punctual (perfective)** present subjunctive [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].

#### Present subjunctive — continuous (imperfective)

Expresses an **ongoing** or **repeated** action in a subordinate context. This form is **unique to Tsakonian** and no longer exists in SMG. It is built by inserting an imperfective morpheme (e.g. _-ίν-_) between the stem and the ending.

<div style="text-align: center">

_Ένι θέου να ορίνου._<br>
*Éni zéou na orínou.*<br>
'I want to keep seeing.' / 'I want to be seeing (continuously).'

</div>

#### Present subjunctive — punctual (perfective)

Expresses a **single, completed** action in a subordinate context. This form corresponds to the SMG present subjunctive and uses the aorist (perfective) stem.

<div style="text-align: center">

_Ένι θέου να οράου._<br>
*Éni zéou na oraoú.*<br>
'I want to see (once).'

</div>

### Imperative mood

The **imperative** gives **direct commands, instructions, and requests**. Like the subjunctive, it distinguishes imperfective (ongoing or repeated) from perfective (single or completed) aspect.

#### Continuous imperative (imperfective)

Commands an **ongoing** or **repeated** action.

<div style="text-align: center">

_Ορήνε!_<br>
*Oríne!*<br>
'Keep looking!' / 'Look (repeatedly)!'

</div>

#### Punctual imperative (perfective)

Commands a **single** action.

<div style="text-align: center">

_Ορά!_<br>
*Orá!*<br>
'Look!'

</div>

### Conditional mood

The **conditional** expresses **hypothetical** or **contrary-to-fact** situations. Formed with _θάκια_ + the subjunctive, it mirrors the future in distinguishing continuous from simple aspect.

#### Conditional continuous

Expresses a **hypothetical ongoing** or **habitual** action. Formed with _θάκια_ + imperfective present subjunctive.

<div style="text-align: center">

_Θάκια ορίνου._<br>
*Zákia orínou.*<br>
'I would be seeing.' / 'I would keep seeing.'

</div>

#### Conditional simple

Expresses a **hypothetical single** or **completed** action. Formed with _θάκια_ + aorist (perfective) subjunctive.

<div style="text-align: center">

_Θάκια οράου._<br>
*Zákia oraoú.*<br>
'I would see.'

</div>

## Voice

Tsakonian distinguishes two voices: the **active**, where the subject performs the action, e.g. _ένι ορού_ (*éni oroú*, 'I see'), and the **passive**, where the subject receives it, e.g. _ένι ορούμενε_ (*éni oroúmene*, 'I am seen'). Each voice has its own forms across the aspectual and temporal range, shown here for the verb _ορού_ (*oroú*, 'to see') [[Aspects of the Passive in Tsakonian.md|(Lysikatos, n.d.)]]:

| Aspect           | Time     | Active voice | Passive voice  |
| ---------------- | -------- | ------------ | -------------- |
| **Imperfective** | Non-past | _ένι ορού_<br>*éni oroú*<br>'I see' | _ένι ορούμενε_<br>*éni oroúmene*<br>'I am seen' |
|                  | Past     | _έμα ορού_<br>*éma oroú*<br>'I was seeing' | _έμα ορούμενε_<br>*éma oroúmene*<br>'I was being seen' |
| **Perfective**   | Non-past | _οραού_<br>*oraoú*<br>'to see (once)' | _οραθού_<br>*orazoú*<br>'to be seen (once)' |
|                  | Past     | _οράκα_<br>*oráka*<br>'I saw' | _οράμα_<br>*oráma*<br>'I was seen' |

### Active

The **active** voice presents the subject as the agent of the action. In the imperfective non-past and past it is periphrastic (_ένι_ / _έμα_ + present participle), e.g. _έμα γράφου_ (*éma gráfou*, 'I was writing'), while the perfective is synthetic, e.g. _οράκα_ (*oráka*, 'I saw'). The full active paradigm of regular verbs is given under [Verb paradigms](#verb-paradigms) below.

### Passive

The **passive** voice presents the subject as the recipient of the action, e.g. _ένι ορούμενε_ (*éni oroúmene*, 'I am seen'). In Tsakonian, passive participles act as full verbs in the clause — in _ένι ορούμενε_ the participle is the main verb, not just a descriptor [[Aspects of the Passive in Tsakonian.md|(Lysikatos, n.d.)]].

- **Imperfective forms** are periphrastic and use a participle ending in _-μένε_ (*-méne*), e.g. _ορούμενε_ (*oroúmene*, 'being seen').
- **Perfective forms** are single words, e.g. _οραθού_ (*orazoú*, 'to be seen once').

## Aspect

**Aspect** is the central organising feature of the Tsakonian verb, cutting across every mood and tense. It distinguishes the **imperfective** (an action seen as ongoing or repeated) from the **perfective** (an action seen as a single, completed whole). The same temporal reference can therefore be expressed two ways: _θα ορίνου_ (*za orínou*, 'I will keep seeing', imperfective) versus _θα οράου_ (*za oraoú*, 'I will see [once]', perfective).

Imperfectivity is marked by morphemes that follow the verb stem. The contrast between the imperfective and perfective subjunctive illustrates this [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]:

| Imperfective | Transliteration | Perfective | Transliteration | Translation |
| ------------ | --------- | ---------- | --------- | ----- |
| _να απρού-κ̔-ου_ | *na aproú-kh-ou* | _να απρού_ | *na aproú* | 'spread' |
| _να ορ-ίν-ου_ | *na or-ín-ou* | _να οράου_ | *na oraoú* | 'see' |

The segments _-κ̔-_ (*-kh-*) and _-ίν-_ (*-ín-*) are imperfective morphemes inserted between the stem and the ending. Tsakonian's retention of a distinct imperfective present subjunctive — lost in Standard Modern Greek — is what makes the pair _να ορίνου_ / _να οράου_ possible at all.

## Gender distinction in periphrastic tenses

A remarkable feature of the periphrastic system is that the participle in the present and imperfect indicative **agrees in gender with the subject** — a feature unique among Greek varieties [[Kostakis Short Grammar of Tsakonian 1951.md|(Kostakis, 1951)]]. A man says _ένι ορού_ (*éni oroú*, 'I see', masc.), but a woman says _ένι ορά_ (*éni orá*, 'I see', fem.):

| Gender | Present ('I want') | Transliteration | Imperfect ('I wanted') | Transliteration |
| ------ | ------------------ | --------- | ---------------------- | --------- |
| Masculine | ένι θέου | *éni zéou* | έμα θέου | *éma zéou* |
| Feminine | ένι θέα | *éni zéa* | έμα θέα | *éma zéa* |
| Neuter | ένι θέουντα | *éni zéounda* | έμα θέουντα | *éma zéounda* |

## Verb paradigms

A **verb paradigm** (or conjugation class) is a group of verbs that share an ending and are conjugated in exactly the same way. Tsakonian verbs fall into two broad conjugations, called **Type A** and **Type B**, defined by the ending of the active verb form: 

* **Type A** ends in unstressed _-ου_ (e.g. _γράφου_, *gráfou*, 'to write'), 
* **Type B** ends in stressed _-ού_ (e.g. _ορού_, *oroú*, 'to see'). 
 
Each type is then subdivided into numbered classes that share a specific ending pattern [[Condensed Grammar of the Tsakonian Language in Tables.md|(García Chaparro, 2023)]].

**How to use the tables below:** identify a verb's class from its present-indicative ending, then attach the class endings shown in each column to the stem to build the aorist, subjunctive, participle, and so on. The periphrastic tenses (present, imperfect, perfect, futures) are then assembled from these parts using the auxiliaries _ένι_ ('to be') and _έχου_ ('to have'), as set out in the [Annex](#annex-full-paradigm-table). For instance, from _γράφου_ (*gráfou*, class PA6) the aorist is _έγραβα_ (*égrava*, 'I wrote') and the perfect participle _γραφτέ_ (*graftέ*, 'written'), which combines with _ένι έχου_ to form the perfect.

### Verb ένι (to be)

The verb _ένι_ (*éni*, 'to be') is the **copula** and the central **auxiliary** of the periphrastic system: it combines with participles to build the present and imperfect of every other verb, e.g. _ένι ορού_ (*éni oroú*, 'I see'). Because the Future Simple normally takes the aorist subjunctive — which _ένι_ lacks — its future and conditional are built on the present subjunctive instead. ### Verb έχου (to have)

Tsakonian expresses 'to have' periphrastically as _ένι έχου_ (literally 'I am having'), corresponding to SMG _έχω_ (*écho*). It is built from the copula _ένι_ plus the participle _έχου_, e.g. _ένι έχου_ (*éni échou*, 'I have'). Crucially, it then serves as the **auxiliary** for the compound past tenses: the perfect and pluperfect of any verb are formed with _ένι έχου_ / _έμα έχου_ + the perfect participle, e.g. _ένι έχου φτατέ_ (*éni échou ftaté*, 'I have arrived') [[Kostakis Short Grammar of Tsakonian 1951.md|(Kostakis, 1951)]].

| Number | Person | Present Indicative | Present Subjunctive | Imperative | Aorist (Imperfect) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sing.** | 1st | ένι έχου (*éni échou*) | έχου (*échou*) | | έμα έχου (*éma échou*) |
| | 2nd | έσι έχου (*ési échou*) | έχερε (*échere*) | έχε (*éche*) | έσα έχου (*ésa échou*) |
| | 3rd | έν̇ι έχου (*én'i échou*) | έχει (*échei*) | | έκι έχου (*éki échou*) |
| **Plur.** | 1st | έμε έχου (*éme échou*) | έχομε (*échome*) | | έμαϊ έχου (*émaï échou*) |
| | 2nd | έτ̔ε έχου (*éthe échou*) | έχετε (*échete*) | | έτ̔αϊ έχου (*éthaï échou*) |
| | 3rd | είνι έχου (*eíni échou*) | έχωι (*échoi*) | | ήγκι έχου (*íngki échou*) |

Its use as an auxiliary across the compound tenses is summarised below:

| Tense          | Formation                    | Example                          | Transliteration |
| -------------- | ---------------------------- | -------------------------------- | --------- |

## Irregular verbs

A small set of high-frequency verbs is **irregular**: their stems change unpredictably between tenses (a phenomenon called **suppletion**), so they cannot be derived from any single paradigm. For example, _αού_ ('I say') has the wholly unrelated aorist _επέκα_ ('I said'), and _παρίου_ ('I come') the aorist _εκάνα_ ('I came'). Most are everyday verbs of motion, perception, and speech [[Condensed Grammar of the Tsakonian Language in Tables.md|(García Chaparro, 2023)]]. The first-person singular is given for each form below.

#### αού (*aoú*, 'to say')

Corresponds to SMG _λέω_. Suppletive aorist stem _επε-_.

| Form | Tsakonian | Transliteration | Translation |
| :--- | :----------- | :----------- | :---- |
| **Present indicative** | ένι αού | *éni aoú* | 'I say' |
| **Imperfect** | έμα αού | *éma aoú* | 'I was saying' |
| **Aorist indicative** | **επέκα** | *epéka* | 'I said' |
| **Present subjunctive** | να αλ̣ήνου | *na al'ínou* | 'that I keep saying' |
| **Aorist subjunctive** | να αλ̣ήου | *na al'íou* | 'that I say' |
| **Imperative (continuous)** | άλ̣ηνε | *ál'ine* | 'keep saying!' |
| **Imperative (simple)** | άλε | *ále* | 'say!' |
| **Perfect participle** | **πετέ** | *peté* | 'said' |
| **Passive present** | ένι αλ̣ικούμενε | *éni al'ikoúmene* | 'I am said' |

## Additional information

### Transitivity marking: the suffix _-αΐχου_

The suffix _-αΐχου_ (*-aíchou*) is widely used to derive transitive (causative) verbs from intransitive bases [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]:

| Intransitive | Transliteration | Translation | Transitive (_-αΐχου_) | Transliteration | Translation |
| ------------ | --------- | ----- | --------------------- | --------- | ----- |
| _μοζού_ | *mozzoú* | 'feel pain' | _μοζαΐχου_ | *mozzaíchou* | 'cause pain' |
| _γερού_ | *geroú* | 'get old' | _γεραΐχου_ | *geraíchou* | 'cause someone to age' |

### Subjunctive vs. indicative: a Doric archaism

Tsakonian is the only Modern Greek dialect that preserves **morphologically distinct endings** in the monolectic (single-word) subjunctive, in contrast to the periphrastic indicative. In SMG the subjunctive has merged completely with the indicative; Tsakonian's distinction — _ένι ορού_ (*éni oroú*, indicative 'I see') vs. _να ορίνου_ (*na orínou*, subjunctive 'that I keep seeing') — is a direct Doric archaism (see [[1.3 Doric heritage]]) [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].

| Voice | Mood | Imperfective | Transliteration | Perfective | Transliteration |
| ----- | ---- | ------------ | --------- | ---------- | --------- |
| **Active** | Indicative | _ένι ορού_ 'I see' | *éni oroú* | — | |
| | Subjunctive | _να ορίνου_ 'that I see' | *na orínou* | _να οράου_ 'that I see' | *na oraoú* |
| **Passive** | Indicative | _ένι ορούμενε_ 'I am seen' | *éni oroúmene* | — | |
| | Subjunctive | _να ορινούμα_ 'that I be seen' | *na orinoúma* | _να οραθού_ 'that I be seen' | *na orazoú* |

The mediopassive present subjunctive mixes the person endings of the ancient subjunctive and optative: _να γραφούμα_ (*na grafoúma*, 'I be written') parallels the ancient optative _γραφοίμην_ (*grafoimín*), while the 3rd person _να γράφηται_ (*na gráfiti*) derives from the ancient subjunctive _γράφηται_ (*gráfiti*) [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].

### The mediopassive aorist and Doric perfect

The mediopassive aorist paradigm is particularly archaic. The ending _-μα_ (*-ma*) corresponds to the _-μην_ (*-min*) of the middle aorist of athematic verbs in Attic, as in _ορά-μα_ (*orá-ma*, 'I was seen') beside Attic _ἐδό-μην_ (*edó-min*) [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]. As past tenses merged, Tsakonian preserved ancient Doric voiced consonants (/v/, /g/) where Athens used breathy sounds — for example _εκρέβα_ (*ekréva*, 'I stole') reflects Doric (Messenian) _κεκλεβώς_ (*keklevós*) rather than Attic _κέκλοφα_ (*kéklopha*).

### Laconian origin of the periphrasis

Contrary to Aerts's (1965) claim that these periphrases are an innovation, verbal periphrases leading to the modern Tsakonian system are already attested in **Hesychius's Laconian glosses** — Hesychius being a 5th-century AD lexicographer who compiled rare Laconian words [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]. The gloss _ἐξηληµβώρ_ (*exilimmór*, 'to perceive'), equivalent to Attic _ἐξειληφώς (ἦν)_, shows that the periphrastic system is an **inherited Laconian feature**, not an independent development. See [[1.3 Doric heritage]] for the broader context of Doric morphological inheritance.

### General note on the augment

In the indicative aorist, an initial **_ε-_** (augment) is added when the verb does not begin with a vowel, e.g. _γράφου_ (*gráfou*, 'to write') → _έγραβα_ (*égrava*, 'I wrote'), but _ορού_ (*oroú*, 'to see') → _οράκα_ (*oráka*, 'I saw') with no added augment [[Condensed Grammar of the Tsakonian Language in Tables.md|(García Chaparro, 2023)]].
