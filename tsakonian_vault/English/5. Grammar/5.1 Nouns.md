Tsakonian nouns are classified by **gender**, **number**, and **case**, though the system has undergone significant simplification and reduction compared to its Ancient Greek roots. While many patterns align with Modern Greek, Tsakonian preserves distinctive archaic forms and unique developments, particularly in its declension classes and the progressive loss of the genitive case.

> **Grammar glossary:**
>
> - **Case**: a grammatical category that marks the role of a noun in a sentence (subject, possessor, object, etc.).
> - **Nominative**: the case marking the subject ('the cat sees').
> - **Genitive**: the case marking possession or relationship ('the cat's tail').
> - **Accusative**: the case marking the direct object ('I see the cat').
> - **Vocative**: the case used for direct address (calling or addressing someone).
> - **Declension**: a pattern of endings that a noun follows.
> - **Gender**: a grammatical property of every noun — masculine, feminine, or neuter — not necessarily related to biological sex, which affects the article and adjective endings the noun takes.

For the Doric historical origin of noun forms, see [[1.3 Doric heritage]]. For how the loss of the genitive affects syntax, see [[5.8 Syntax]] and [[5.7 Prepositions and Postpositions]].

## Cases

The Tsakonian case system primarily consists of three functional cases: **nominative**, **genitive**, and **accusative**, plus a **vocative** of direct address. The ancient **dative** has been entirely lost; see [Additional information](#additional-information) for how its functions are now expressed.

| Case           | Primary Function                      | Status                                     |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Nominative** | Subject of the sentence               | Fully functional                           |
| **Genitive**   | Possession, relationship              | Weakening; near-total loss in plural       |
| **Accusative** | Direct object, object of prepositions | Fully functional; often replacing genitive |
| **Vocative**   | Direct address                        | Not distinctly marked                       |

### Nominative case

The nominative marks the **subject** of a sentence — the person or thing performing the action. It is also the **citation form**: the form under which a noun is listed in dictionaries and word lists.

Its main usages are:

- **Subject of a verb** — the agent or topic the sentence is about:

<div style="text-align: center">

_Ο άθρωπο_<br>
*O ázropo*<br>
'The man'

</div>

- **Predicate noun** — the complement of the copula 'to be', which also stands in the nominative.

### Genitive case

The genitive marks **possession and relationship** ('the cat's tail', 'the door of the house'). It is the most reduced of the functional cases. The genitive **plural** has almost entirely disappeared from spoken Tsakonian. [[About the Genitive in Tsakonian Nouns.md|(Kisilier & Mertyris, 2018)]]

In singular, it is still productive in the singular for many nouns:

<div style="text-align: center">

_Ταρ αγάκη_<br>
*Tar agáki*<br>
'Of love'

</div>

<div style="text-align: center">

_Του βράχου_<br>
*Tou vráchou*<br>
'Of the rock'

</div>

The genitive is undergoing progressive reduction and replacement. While functional in the singular for many nouns, it has nearly vanished in the plural.

Its decline is uneven across noun types:

- **Neuter nouns in -άτου (*-átou*) and -ίου (*-íou*):** Modern speakers have almost entirely lost these forms (e.g., _σωμάτου_ (*somátou*), _μαλίου_ (*malíou*) are rarely used).
- **Feminine nouns in -έ (*-é*):** Distinct forms like _κ̔αρέ_ (*kharé*, 'fire') often coexist with uninflected variants (_κ̔άρα_, *khára*) or have disappeared entirely.
- **Variation:** Some nouns show regularization toward the **-ου** (*-ou*) pattern or outright loss of endings (e.g., _πέτσ̌ε_ (*pétshe*) → gen. _πέτσ̌ε_ (*pétshe*)).

#### Plural genitive

The plural genitive is restricted to a few fixed expressions of age, quantity, or value listed below [[About the Genitive in Tsakonian Nouns.md|(Kisilier & Mertyris, 2018)]]. Everywhere else, possession is expressed through alternative constructions.

<div style="text-align: center">

_Δέκα χρονού καμπζί_<br>
*Déka chronoú kampzzí*<br>
'A ten-year-old child'

</div>

<div style="text-align: center">

_Δύ' μηνού καμπζί_<br>
*Dý' minoú kampzzí*<br>
'A two-month-old child'

</div>

| Nominative | Gen. Plural | Transliteration | Translation |
| ---------- | ----------- | --------------- | ----------- |
| αμέρα      | αμερού      | *améra, ameroú* | 'day'      |
| γίδα       | γιδού       | *gída, gidoú* | 'goat'     |
| δραχουμή   | δραχουμού   | *drachoumí, drachoumoú* | 'drachma'  |
| εριφό      | εριφού      | *erifó, erifoú* | 'kid'      |
| μήνα       | μηνού       | *mína, minoú* | 'month'    |
| χρόνε      | χρονού      | *chróne, chronoú* | 'year'     |
| ψαλία      | ψαλιδού     | *psalía, psalidoú* | 'scissors' |

#### Periphrastic alternatives

Where the genitive is no longer used, possession is increasingly expressed via two main strategies [[About the Genitive in Tsakonian Nouns.md|(Kisilier & Mertyris, 2018)]]:

1.  **Accusative replacement:** _οι πούε τα κότ̒α_ (*oi poúe ta kótha*, 'the feet of the hen').
2.  **Prepositional phrases:**
    - **With _σε_ ('to/in'):** _οι ψιλοί σ' έταν̇η τα γουναίκα_ (*oi psiloí s' étan'i ta gounaíka*, 'the eyes of this woman'). Coalescence occurs before the article: _οι ψιλοί τ̒ουρ αθρίποι_ (*oi psiloí tiour azrípoi*, 'the eyes of the people').
    - **With _από_ ('from'):** _οι πούε από τα καμπζία_ (*oi poúe apó ta kampzzía*, 'the feet of the children').

### Accusative case

The accusative marks the **direct object** of a verb and the **object of most prepositions**. As the genitive recedes, the accusative increasingly takes over its functions as well. The lost dative (indirect object) is now expressed with the preposition _σε_ ('to') followed by the accusative.

Its main usages are:

- **Direct object** — the entity directly affected by the action:

<div style="text-align: center">

_Ένι δίου το βιβλίε_<br>
*Éni díou to vivlíe*<br>
'I give the book'

</div>

- **Indirect object** — expressed by _σε_ + accusative, replacing the old dative:

<div style="text-align: center">

_Τ̔αν κοπέα_<br>
*Than kopéa*<br>
'To the girl'

</div>

- **Object of a preposition:**

<div style="text-align: center">

_Από τα καμπζία_<br>
*Apó ta kampzzía*<br>
'From the children'

</div>

- **Replacing the genitive** — the accusative is commonly used in place of a genitive of possession: _οι πούε τα κότ̒α_ (*oi poúe ta kótha*, 'the feet of the hen').

## Gender

Tsakonian has three genders: **masculine**, **feminine**, and **neuter**. Gender is not necessarily tied to biological sex; it is a grammatical property of every noun, marked by the definite article, the noun ending, and agreement with adjectives [[About the Genitive in Tsakonian Nouns.md|(Kisilier & Mertyris, 2018)]].

### Definite article by gender

The clearest marker of a noun's gender is the article that accompanies it:

| Case           | Masculine | Feminine                  | Neuter |
| -------------- | --------- | ------------------------- | ------ |
| **Nominative** | ο (*o*) | α (*a*) | το (*to*) |
| **Genitive**   | του (*tou*) | ταρ (*tar*) (vowels) / τα (*ta*) (cons.) | του (*tou*) |
| **Accusative** | τον (*ton*) | ταν (*tan*) (vowels) / τα (*ta*) (cons.) | το (*to*) |

The feminine article variants result from **rhotacism** (the sound change /s/ → /r/; see [[2. Phonology]]). The behaviour depends on the following sound [[About the Genitive in Tsakonian Nouns.md|(Kisilier & Mertyris, 2018)]]:

- **Before vowels:** _τας_ (*tas*) → _ταρ_ (*tar*) (e.g., _ταρ αγάκη_ (*tar agáki*) 'of love'). This preserves a phonological distinction between genitive and accusative.
- **Before consonants:** the final /s/ of the genitive article and the final /n/ of the accusative article both assimilate or are lost, causing the cases to collapse into **syncretism** — the merging of two formerly distinct forms into one (e.g., _α νύ[ι]θη_ (*a ný[i]zi*) → gen./acc. _τα νύ[ι]θη_ (*ta ný[i]zi*)).

### Masculine nouns

Masculine nouns take the article _ο / του / τον_ and end chiefly in **-α** (ancient 1st declension) or in **-ε / -ο / -έ / -ό** (ancient 2nd declension). Their declension classes are labelled **A1–A5** by [[Condensed Grammar of the Tsakonian Language in Tables.md|(García Chaparro, 2023)]], according mainly to the plural ending.

Masculine plurals draw a key **animacy distinction** — whether the referent is living or not [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]:

- **Animate** nouns take **-οι** (*-oi*): _αθρίποι_ (*azrípoi*, 'people'), _υζοί_ (*yzzoí*, 'sons').
- **Inanimate** nouns take **-ου** (*-ou*): _τόπου_ (*tópou*, 'places'), _ακ̔ού_ (*akhoú*, 'vessels').

#### Paradigm A1 — Ending in -α, plural in -οι

Animate nouns ending in **-α**, with a genitive also in **-α** (distinguished mainly by the article), and a plural in **-οι**. Genitives in **-ου** (marked with an asterisk) likely result from Standard Modern Greek influence.

| Nominative  | Genitive         | Plural      | Transliteration | Translation  |
| ----------- | ---------------- | ----------- | --------------- | ------------ |
| ο κ̔αβουρα     | του κ̔αβουρα        | κ̔αβουροι      | *o khávoura, tou khávoura, khávouroi* | crab      |
| ο σ̌ίνα      | του σ̌ίνου\*      | σ̌ίν̇οι       | *o shína, tou shínou, shín'oi* | mountain     |
| ο Σπαρτιάτα | του Σπαρτιατού\* | Σπαρτιάτοι  | *o Spartiáta, tou Spartiatoú, Spartiátoi* | Spartan      |

#### Paradigm A2 — ο/ε-type (ancient 2nd declension)

The most productive masculine pattern: nominatives in **-ο / -ό / -ε / -έ** with a genitive in **-ου / -ού** [[About the Genitive in Tsakonian Nouns.md|(Kisilier & Mertyris, 2018)]]. The plural is **-οι** for animates and **-ου** for inanimates. One representative of each subtype:

| Subtype           | Nominative | Genitive     | Transliteration | Translation  |
| ----------------- | ---------- | ------------ | --------------- | ------------ |
| **-ο (disyll.)**  | ο βράχο    | του βράχου   | *o vrácho, tou vráchou* | rock         |
| **-ό (disyll.)**  | ο αγό      | του αγού     | *o agó, tou agoú* | hare         |
| **-ο (trisyll.)** | ο άθρωπο   | του αθρούπου | *o ázropo, tou azroúpou* | man          |
| **-ε**            | ο ξένε     | του ξένου    | *o xéne, tou xénou* | stranger     |
| **-έ**            | ο υζέ      | του υζού     | *o yzzé, tou yzzoú* | son          |

#### Paradigm A3 — Ending in -α, plural in -ου

Inanimate nouns of the same α-type, whose plural ends in **-ου** rather than the animate **-οι**.

| Nominative  | Genitive         | Plural      | Transliteration | Translation  |
| ----------- | ---------------- | ----------- | --------------- | ------------ |
| ο όντα      | του όντα         | όντου       | *o ónta, tou ónta, óntou* | tooth            |

#### Paradigm A4 — ε-type with -ούνε plural

A small group of nouns ending in **-ε** whose plural is formed by adding **-νε** to the genitive stem in **-ου**, yielding a plural in **-ούνε**. Unlike A2, this plural is not conditioned by animacy — it is lexically specific.

| Nominative | Genitive  | Plural  | Transliteration | Translation |
| ---------- | --------- | ------- | --------------- | ----------- |
| ο άγιε     | του αγίου | αγίουνε | *o ágie, tou agíou, agíoune* | church |

#### Paradigm A5 — uninflected genitive, -δε plural

A set of nouns whose genitive is **identical to the nominative** (no dedicated genitive ending) and whose plural is formed by appending **-δε** directly to the nominative form. [[Condensed Grammar of the Tsakonian Language in Tables.md|(García Chaparro, 2023)]]:

| Ending | Nominative | Genitive | Plural   | Transliteration | Translation |
| ------- | ---------- | -------- | -------- | --------------- | ----------- |
| **-ά**  | ο ψαρά     | ψαρά     | ψαράδε   | *o psará, psará, psaráde* | fisherman |
| **-ία** | ο βορία    | βορία    | βοριάδε  | *o voría, voría, voriáde* | north wind |
| **-η**  | ο αφέγκη   | αφέγκη   | αφέγκηδε | *o afégki, afégki, afégkide* | father |
| **-έ**  | ο καφέ     | καφέ     | καφέδε   | *o kafé, kafé, kaféde* | coffee |
| **-ού** | ο παππού   | παππού   | παππούδε | *o pappoú, pappoú, pappoúde* | grandfather |

#### Irregular masculine nouns

A small group continues the ancient 3rd declension or otherwise departs from the regular paradigms. Their genitives are built on a different stem from the nominative, which is what makes them irregular [[About the Genitive in Tsakonian Nouns.md|(Kisilier & Mertyris, 2018)]].

| Nominative | Genitive   | Plural | Transliteration | Translation | Why irregular |
| ---------- | ---------- | ------ | --------------- | ----------- | ------------- |
| ο κούε     | του κουνέ  | κούν̇οι (*koún'oi*) | *o koúe, tou kouné* | dog   | Genitive on the stem *κουν-* < ancient κύων → κυνός |
| ο ούθι     | του ουθίου | ουθίουνε (*ouzíoúne*) | *o oúzi, tou ouzíou* | snake | Adds *-ίου* on a 3rd-declension stem < ὄφις → ὄφεος |
| ο μήνα     | του μηνέ   | μην̇οι (*mín'oi*) | *o mína, tou miné* | month | Genitive on the stem *μην-*; ancient 3rd declension |
| ο ψιλέ     | του ψιού   | ψιλ̣οί (*psil'oí*) | *o psilé, tou psioú* | eye | Stem-final /l/ lost in the genitive |
| ο αθή      | του αθή    | αθήνε (*azíne*) | *o azí, tou azí* | brother | Adds the distinctively Tsakonian *-νε* plural |
| ο γέρου    | του γέρου  | γέροι (*géroi*) | *o gérou, tou gérou* | old man | Invariable nominative/genitive in *-ου* |

> **Note:** _γούνα_ (*goúna*, 'knee') shows **gender mobility**: it may be used as either masculine or neuter. As a masculine noun it takes the animate plural **γούν̇οι** (*goún'oi*); as a neuter it takes the ancient consonant-stem plural **γούνατα** (*goúnata*) [[Dictionary of the Tsakonian Dialect Volume II.md|(Kostakis, 1986b)]]. See also the Irregular neuter nouns section.

### Feminine nouns

Feminine nouns take the article _α / ταρ ~ τα / ταν ~ τα_ and end chiefly in **-α** (some in **-η** or **-ού**). Their declension classes are labelled **Θ1–Θ5** by [[Condensed Grammar of the Tsakonian Language in Tables.md|(García Chaparro, 2023)]], distinguished primarily by the **plural ending**. Before consonants the genitive and accusative collapse (syncretism); before vowels, rhotacism keeps them distinct (_ταρ_ vs. _ταν_).

#### Paradigm Θ1 — Ending in -α or -η, plural in -ε

The most productive feminine class. The genitive is marked by the article alone unless a secondary form in **-έ** or **-ή** is also present (see [Secondary genitive patterns](#secondary-genitive-patterns)).

| Nominative | Genitive | Plural | Transliteration | Translation |
| ---------- | -------- | ------ | --------------- | ----------- |
| α πούα     | τα πούα  | πούε   | *a poúa, ta poúa, poúe* | foot |

#### Paradigm Θ2 — Ending in -α, plural in -λε

The plural replaces the final vowel with **-λε**.

| Nominative | Genitive  | Plural | Transliteration | Translation |
| ---------- | --------- | ------ | --------------- | ----------- |
| α τσ̑έα    | τα τσ̑ελή | τσ̑έλε | *a tsséa, ta tsselí, tsséle* | house |

#### Paradigm Θ3 — Ending in -ά, plural in -άε

Nouns with a stressed **-ά** nominative. The plural appends **-ε** to the nominative form.

| Nominative | Genitive   | Plural   | Transliteration | Translation |
| ---------- | ---------- | -------- | --------------- | ----------- |
| α μελισσά  | τα μελισσά | μελισσάε | *a melissá, ta melissá, melissáe* | bee |

#### Paradigm Θ4 — Ending in -ά or -α, plural in -αδε

The plural adds **-δε** to the nominative stem.

| Nominative | Genitive  | Plural   | Transliteration | Translation |
| ---------- | --------- | -------- | --------------- | ----------- |
| α γιορτά   | τα γιορτά | γιορτάδε | *a giortá, ta giortá, giortáde* | party |

#### Paradigm Θ5 — Ending in -ου, plural in -ουδε

Nouns ending in **-ου**, with a genitive identical to the nominative.

| Nominative | Genitive  | Plural   | Transliteration | Translation |
| ---------- | --------- | -------- | --------------- | ----------- |
| α αλεπού   | τα αλεπού | αλεπούδε | *a alepoú, ta alepoú, alepoúde* | female fox |

### Neuter nouns

Neuter nouns take the article _το / του / το_ (nominative and accusative are always identical). Their declension classes are labelled **Υ1–Υ5** by [[Condensed Grammar of the Tsakonian Language in Tables.md|(García Chaparro, 2023)]], distinguished primarily by the **plural ending**.

#### Paradigm Υ1 — plural in -α

| το γάστενε | του γαστένου | τα γάστενα | *to gástene, tou gasténou, ta gástena* | chestnut |
| το σίδερε  | του σιδέρου | τα σίδερα  | *to sídere, tou sidérou, ta sídera* | iron |
| το χόντι   | του χόντου   | τα χόντα   | *to chónti, tou chóntou, ta chónta* | grass |

#### Paradigm Υ2 — plural in -ματα or -ιματα

Derived from ancient 3rd-declension *-ματος* stems: the genitive ends in **-μάτου** or **-ιμάτου** and the plural in **-ματα** or **-ιματα**.

| Nominative | Genitive | Plural | Transliteration | Translation |
| ---------- | -------- | ------ | --------------- | ----------- |
| το σώμα    | του σωμάτου   | τα σώματα     | *to sóma, tou somátou, ta sómata* | body |
| το χώμα    | του χωμάτου   | τα χώματα     | *to chóma, tou chomátou, ta chómata* | earth/soil |
| το πούμα   | του πουμάτου  | τα πούματα    | *to poúma, tou poumátou, ta poúmata* | lid/cover |
| το γράψιμο | του γραψιμάτου | τα γραψίματα | *to grápsimo, tou grapsimátou, ta grapsímata* | writing |
| το σ̌άψιμο  | του σ̌αψιμάτου | τα σ̌αψίματα  | *to shápsimo, tou shapsimátou, ta shapsímata* | sewing |

One further archaic neuter preserves an ancient consonant stem with the same **-άτου** genitive: _το γούνα / του γουνάτου_ (*to goúna, tou gounátou*, 'knee') < γόνατος.

#### Paradigm Υ3 — plural in -ια

The genitive ends in **-ίου**; the plural adds **-α** to give **-ια**.

## Additional information

### The lost dative case

The **dative** case (marking indirect objects, e.g. 'to the cat') has been entirely lost. Instead, the indirect object is expressed using a preposition (usually _σε_, 'to') followed by the accusative case. For example, to say 'I give the book to the girl', you say _ένι δίου το βιβλίε τ̔αν κοπέα_ (*éni díou to vivlíe than kopéa*). Early records (e.g., Oikonomos, 1870) listed dative forms, but these were either accusatives with a preposition or errors influenced by **Katharevousa** — the archaic formal register of Greek used in official contexts until the 1970s, which artificially preserved ancient forms not present in spoken language [[About the Genitive in Tsakonian Nouns.md|(Kisilier & Mertyris, 2018)]].

### Number and case in the plural

Tsakonian plural formation is largely **uninflected for case** in Peloponnesian dialects: a single plural form generally serves all syntactic roles [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].

One exception appears among **semi-speakers** of the northern subdialect, who sometimes restore **-ου** (*-ou*) as an accusative plural marker (e.g., nom. pl. _οι ελάφοι_ (*oi eláfoi*) vs. acc. pl. _τιρ ελάφου_ (*tir eláfou*)), reintroducing a case distinction into an otherwise syncretic paradigm [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].

### Modern Greek influence on the genitive

Several α-type masculine nouns show a genitive in **-ου** (e.g., _του σ̌ίνου_, _του Σπαρτιατού_) where the inherited form would end in **-α**. These **-ου** genitives likely result from Standard Modern Greek influence rather than native Tsakonian development [[About the Genitive in Tsakonian Nouns.md|(Kisilier & Mertyris, 2018)]].
