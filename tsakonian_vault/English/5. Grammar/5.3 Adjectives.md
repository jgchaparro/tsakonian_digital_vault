Tsakonian adjectives agree with the noun they modify in **gender**, **number**, and **case** — that is, their endings change to match those of the noun. Tsakonian adjectives are classified into several types based on their gender-specific endings and declension patterns. This section is primarily based on the systematic tables provided by [[Condensed Grammar of the Tsakonian Language in Tables.md|(García Chaparro, 2023)]] and the short grammar of [[Kostakis Short Grammar of Tsakonian 1951.md|Kostakis (1951)]].

For the case and gender system used in the table headers, see [[5.1 Nouns]]. For the overall grammar structure, see the [[5. Grammar]] index.

## Rules differentiating the paradigms

### 1. Accent position

Accent position is the primary criterion for assigning an adjective to one of the three major groups. Kostakis organises his grammar around exactly this division.

**Oxytone (accent on the final syllable) → E1 and E5**

These are the most common adjectives. The masculine and neuter share the same ending; the feminine differs. In E1 the masculine/neuter nominative ends in **-ε** and the feminine in **-α**, mirroring the standard 2-1-2 Greek pattern. Examples from Kostakis: *μονέ / μονά / μονέ* (= μονός), *καλέ / καλά / καλέ*, *ξανθέ*, *χρυσέ*, *κρυανέ*.

In E5 the masculine/neuter end in **-ο** instead of -ε. Examples: *ἀρνικό / ἀρνικά / ἀρνικό* (= ἀρνήσιος), *ὄμορφο / ὄμορφα / ὄμορφο* (= ὄμορφος). This type is more common in the Propontis variety (V).

Both E1 and E5 form the genitive in **-ου** and the masculine plural in **-οι** (often spelled -ού in Tsakonian orthography due to the merger of ο/ου).

**Paroxytone (accent on the penultimate syllable) → E7**

E7 continues the ancient Greek -ύς / -εῖα / -ύ adjectives. The masculine and neuter end in **-ιού** and the feminine in **-ία**, all with the accent on the second-to-last syllable. The masculine plural is **-οί**, the feminine plural **-ίε**, the neuter plural **-ία**.

Examples from Kostakis: *βαθίε / βαθία / βαθίου* (= βαθύς), *βαρίε / βαρία / βαρριού* (= βαρύς), *πρατίε / πρατία / πρακιού* (= πλατύς), *μακρίε / μακρία / μακριού* (= μακρύς).

Several adjectives that are not etymologically -ύς have been pulled into this paradigm by analogy: *ροῦσε / ρούσα / ρούσου* (= ξανθός), *ποῖε / ποία / ποίου* (= ποιός), *πᾶσε / πᾶσα / πάσου* (= πᾶς/ὅλος).

**Proparoxytone (accent on the antepenultimate syllable) → E2, E3, E4**

These continue the ancient -ιος / -ια / -ιον adjectives. The neuter invariably ends in **-ιου** (= -ιον > -ιο > -ιου), and the genitive is **-ε** (same form as the masculine nominative). The distinction between E2, E3 and E4 lies in the feminine ending and in consonant behaviour (see §3 below):

- **E2**: masculine and feminine differ — masc. **-ε**, fem. **-α** — because the stem allows a distinct feminine. Examples: *ἕμισε / ἕμισα / ἕμισου* (= ἥμισυς), *δίτσε / δίτσα / δίτσου* (= δίκιος; the feminine also occurs as *δίτσα* by analogy with the koine).
- **E3**: masculine and feminine are identical (**-ε**). This is the typical outcome when the stem ends in a palatal cluster or when no distinct feminine suffix developed. Examples: *ντόπιε / ντόπιε / ντόπιου* (= ντόπιος), *τέτοιε / τέτοιε / τέτοιου* (= τέτοιος), *ἀνάλατε / ἀνάλατε / ἀνάλατου* (= ἀνάλατος). The feminine occasionally takes **-α** under koine influence: *ντόπια*.
- **E4**: like E3 (masc. = fem. **-ε**, neut. **-ιου**) but the genitive is not attested or not productive for the specific items in this subtype. In practice E4 and E3 are often conflated.

### 2. Genitive ending

The genitive is a reliable secondary criterion:

| Genitive | Types |
|:---------|:------|
| **-ου** | E1, E5, E6 (oxytone and quasi-oxytone stems) |
| **-ε**  | E2, E3 (proparoxytone -ιος stems) |
| not distinct | E7 (the neuter nom./acc. -ιού serves as genitive), E4 (no distinct genitive recorded) |

---

### 3. Consonant mutations in the neuter

The neuter of E7 (the -ύς class) and of some E3 adjectives shows consonant alternations at the stem-suffix boundary. These are the same mutations found in the Tsakonian noun system.

**ρ → ζ before -ου**

When the adjective stem ends in *-ρ* and the neuter suffix is *-ου* (whether from -υ or from another -ου neuter), the *ρ* shifts to **ζ**:

| Masc. | Fem. | Neut. | Greek equivalent |
|:------|:-----|:------|:----------------|
| βαρίε | βαρία | βαρ**ρι**ού | βαρύς |
| μακρίε | μακρία | μακ**ρι**ού | μακρύς |
| δεύτερε | δεύτερε | δεύτε**ζ**ου | δεύτερος |

In *μακρύς*, the cluster *-κρ-* before *-ού* is simplified: the *ρ* becomes *ζ*, yielding *μακριού*.

**τ → κ before -ι-**

When the stem ends in *-τ* and the neuter suffix begins with *-ι-* (as in -ιου), the *τ* palatalises to **κ**:

| Masc. | Fem. | Neut. | Greek equivalent |
|:------|:-----|:------|:----------------|
| πρατίε | πρατία | πρα**κ**ιού | πλατύς |
| πρώτε | πρώτα | πρώ**κ**ιου | πρῶτος |
| τεσσάρατε | τεσσάρατε | τεσσάρα**κ**ιου | τέταρτος |

The same mutation is visible in ordinal numerals and in several pronouns whose neuter was remodelled on the -ιου adjectives (e.g. *τόδου* < *τοῦτο* → τοῦτ + ιου → τόδ/τότ + κιου).

**λ → absent or → ζ**

Tsakonian regularly loses final or pre-consonantal *λ*. In adjective stems this means stems in *-λ-* may show elision or a reflex *ζ* in the neuter, though adjective examples of this are rarer than in nouns.

### 4. Feminine ending

The feminine ending encodes a secondary distinction within each accent group:

| Feminine ending | Types | Notes |
|:----------------|:------|:------|
| **-α** | E1, E2, E5, E8 | Default outcome; from -α or -η (koine) stems |
| **-ε** (= masc.) | E3, E4 | No distinct feminine; typical of -ιος stems where the fem. -ια- merged with the masc. |
| **-ο** | E6 | Unusual; a small class with a round-vowel feminine |
| **-ία** | E7 | From ancient -εῖα; always stressed |

---

### 5. E6 and E8: special classes

**E6** (*-ε / -ο / -ε*, gen. *-ου*) constitutes a small class where the feminine ends in **-ο** instead of **-α**. The plural feminine is **-οι** (identical to the masculine plural), which distinguishes it from all other types. The exact membership of this class is not fully documented in Kostakis.

**E8** (*-η / -α / -ικο*) is a highly irregular type. The masculine ends in **-η** (from an ancient -ης stem), the neuter in **-ικο** with genitive **-ικου**, and the masculine plural in **-ηδε** (a characteristically Tsakonian plural in *-δε*). This class derives from agent-noun adjectives in -ης/-ιτης that were integrated into the adjectival system. The most cited example is *μιτσή / μιτσά / μιτσί* (= μικρός, small), where the neuter *μιτσί* is formed on the -ι neuter pattern rather than the -ικο, suggesting E8 covers a heterogeneous residual group.

---

### 6. Comparative and superlative

Kostakis emphasises that monolexical comparatives are much more productive in Tsakonian than in the koine. The comparative suffix **-ούτερε / -ουτέρα / -ούτεξου** (or *-ούτερε / -ουτέρα / -ούτερου* for stems in *-ρ*) is added to the positive stem and declines as a proparoxytone adjective (E3 pattern):

| Positive | Comparative (M) | Comparative (F) | Comparative (N) |
|:---------|:----------------|:----------------|:----------------|
| ἁτσέ (= μεγάλος) | ἁτσύτερε | ἁτσυτέρα | ἁτούτεξου |
| μιτσή (= μικρός) | μιτσούτερε | μιτσουτέρα | μιτσούτεξου |
| ναἱέ (= ὥριμος) | ναἱούτερε | ναἱουτέρα | ναἱούτεξου |
| χαμηλέ | χαμηότερε | χαμηότερα | χαμηότερε |

Note the **-ξου** neuter, a variant of **-ζου** caused by the same *ρ → ζ* mutation: the comparative stem ends in *-τερ-*, so *-τερ + ου → -τεζ/ξου*.

The superlative is periphrastic: the particle *τελεία* (= absolutely, totally) or the construction *ἀπὸ οὕλου / οὕλε / οὕλα* (= of all) precedes the comparative. A monolexical superlative in *-τατε* (as cited by Deffner) is not genuinely Tsakonian according to Kostakis.
