Tsakonian adjectives agree with the noun they modify in **gender**, **number**, and **case** — their endings change to match those of the noun. The same set of endings is reused across the three genders, so learning the noun system first (see [[5.1 Nouns]]) makes the adjective endings largely predictable.

> **For English speakers:** In English, adjectives never change (*a big man*, *big women*). In Tsakonian, they do: *ατσ̌έ άθρωπο* (big man) but *ατσ̌ά γουναίκα* (big woman). Think of it like English *he / she / it* — the form shifts depending on what it refers to.

> **Grammar glossary:**
>
> - **Agreement**: the requirement that an adjective copy the gender, number, and case of the noun it modifies.
> - **Gender**: a noun's grammatical class — masculine, feminine, or neuter — which selects the adjective ending.
> - **Number**: singular or plural.
> - **Case**: the grammatical role of the phrase (subject, possessor, object); see [[5.1 Nouns]].
> - **Oxytone**: a word stressed on the **last** syllable (*καλέ*).
> - **Paroxytone**: a word stressed on the **second-to-last** syllable (*κουβάνε*).
> - **Proparoxytone**: a word stressed on the **third-to-last** syllable (*ντόκιε*).
> - **Paradigm (declension class)**: a fixed pattern of endings; Tsakonian adjectives are grouped into classes labelled **E1–E6**.
> - **Positive / Comparative / Superlative**: the three degrees of comparison ('big' / 'bigger' / 'biggest').

This section is based on [[Condensed Grammar of the Tsakonian Language in Tables.md|(García Chaparro, 2023)]] and [[Kostakis Short Grammar of Tsakonian 1951.md|Kostakis (1951)]].

For the case and gender system, see [[5.1 Nouns]]. For the overall grammar structure, see the [[5. Grammar]] index.

## Case inflection

Like nouns, adjectives inflect for **case**, but they only ever appear attached to a noun, so their case is dictated by agreement rather than chosen independently. An adjective takes whatever case its head noun is in:

- **Nominative** — when the noun is the subject or a predicate complement: _ο μονέ άθρωπο_ (*o moné ázropo*, 'the only man').
- **Accusative** — when the noun is a direct object or governed by a preposition: _ένι ορού το μονέ άθρωπο_ (*éni oroú to moné ázropo*, 'I see the only man'). For most adjectives the accusative is identical to the nominative.
- **Genitive** — marking possession or relationship. As with nouns, the adjectival genitive is **strongly reduced**: it survives mainly in the **singular** and depends on the paradigm — most types form it in **-ου** (E1, E2, E6), while E3 and E5 form it in **-ε**. The E4 class has no distinct genitive.
- **Plural** — Tsakonian plurals are largely **caseless**: a single plural form (M **-οι**, F **-οι**, N **-α** for most types) serves every syntactic role.

Because adjective case simply tracks the noun, the tables below list each paradigm by its **nominative/accusative**, **genitive**, and **plural** forms.

## Paradigms

Adjective classes are labelled **E1–E6** by [[Condensed Grammar of the Tsakonian Language in Tables.md|(García Chaparro, 2023)]]. The most reliable clue for assigning an adjective to a class is **where the stress falls** on the masculine form, so the living, productive types are organised below by accent: **oxytone**, **paroxytone**, and **proparoxytone**.

### Oxytone

Oxytone adjectives carry the stress on the **last syllable**. They are the most common type. The masculine and neuter share the same ending, while the feminine differs; the genitive is **-ού** and the masculine plural **-οί**. The choice between the two oxytone classes depends on the **consonant before the ending**.

#### Paradigm E1 — Ending in -έ, -ά, -έ

Used when the consonant preceding the ending is **ν, δ, τ, λ, or ρ**.

**Endings:**

| Form | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter |
| ---- | --------- | -------- | ------ |
| **Nom. / Acc.** | -έ (*-é*) | -ά (*-á*) | -έ (*-é*) |
| **Genitive** | -ού (*-oú*) | -ά (*-á*) | -ού (*-oú*) |
| **Plural** | -οί (*-oí*) | -οί (*-oí*) | -ά (*-á*) |

Example: _μονέ_ (*moné*, 'only').

| Form | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter |
| ---- | --------- | -------- | ------ |
| **Nom. / Acc.** | μονέ (*moné*) | μονά (*moná*) | μονέ (*moné*) |
| **Genitive** | μονού (*monoú*) | μονά (*moná*) | μονού (*monoú*) |
| **Plural** | μον̇οί (*mon'oí*) | μον̇οί (*mon'oí*) | μονά (*moná*) |

#### Paradigm E2 — Ending in -ό, -ά, -ό

Used when the preceding consonant is **any other**.

**Endings:**

| Form | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter |
| ---- | --------- | -------- | ------ |
| **Nom. / Acc.** | -ό (*-ó*) | -ά (*-á*) | -ό (*-ó*) |
| **Genitive** | -ού (*-oú*) | -ά (*-á*) | -ού (*-oú*) |
| **Plural** | -οί (*-oí*) | -οί (*-oí*) | -ά (*-á*) |

Example: _κακό_ (*kakó*, 'bad').

| Form | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter |
| ---- | --------- | -------- | ------ |
| **Nom. / Acc.** | κακό (*kakó*) | κακά (*kaká*) | κακό (*kakó*) |
| **Genitive** | κακού (*kakoú*) | κακά (*kaká*) | κακού (*kakoú*) |
| **Plural** | κακοί (*kakoí*) | κακοί (*kakoí*) | κακά (*kaká*) |

### Paroxytone

Paroxytone adjectives carry the stress on the **second-to-last syllable**.

#### Paradigm E3 — Ending in -ε, -α, -ιου

**Endings:**

| Form | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter |
| ---- | --------- | -------- | ------ |
| **Nom. / Acc.** | -ε (*-e*) | -α (*-a*) | -ιου (*-iou*) |
| **Genitive** | -ε (*-e*) | -α (*-a*) | -ιου (*-iou*) |
| **Plural** | -οι (*-oi*) | -οι (*-oi*) | -α (*-a*) |

Example: _κουβάνε_ (*kouváne*, 'black').

| Form | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter |
| ---- | --------- | -------- | ------ |
| **Nom. / Acc.** | κουβάνε (*kouváne*) | κουβάνα (*kouvána*) | κουβάνιου (*kouvániou*) |
| **Genitive** | κουβάνε (*kouváne*) | κουβάνα (*kouvána*) | κουβάνιου (*kouvániou*) |
| **Plural** | κουβάν̇οι (*kouván'oi*) | κουβάν̇οι (*kouván'oi*) | κουβάνα (*kouvána*) |

#### Paradigm E4 — Ending in -ίου, -ία, -ίου

This class continues the ancient Greek **-ύς / -εῖα / -ύ** adjectives.

**Endings:**

| Form | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter |
| ---- | --------- | -------- | ------ |
| **Nom. / Acc.** | -ίου (*-íou*) | -ία (*-ía*) | -ίου (*-íou*) |
| **Genitive** | — | — | — |
| **Plural** | -οί (*-oí*) | -ίε (*-íe*) | -ία (*-ía*) |

Example: _βαθίου_ (*vathíou*, 'deep').

| Form | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter |
| ---- | --------- | -------- | ------ |
| **Nom. / Acc.** | βαθίου (*vathíou*) | βαθία (*vathía*) | βαθίου (*vathíou*) |
| **Genitive** | — | — | — |
| **Plural** | βαθιοί (*vathioí*) | βαθίε (*vathíe*) | βαθία (*vathía*) |

Others of this type: _βαρίου_ (heavy), _πρατίου_ (wide), _μακρίου_ (long). A few adjectives outside the ancient class have joined E4 by analogy, e.g. _ρούσου_ (blond), _πάσου_ (every / all).

### Proparoxytone

Proparoxytone adjectives carry the stress on the **third-to-last syllable**.

#### Paradigm E5 — Ending in -ε, -ε, -ε

**Endings:**

| Form | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter |
| ---- | --------- | -------- | ------ |
| **Nom. / Acc.** | -ε (*-e*) | -ε (*-e*) | -ε (*-e*) |
| **Genitive** | -ε (*-e*) | -ε (*-e*) | -ε (*-e*) |
| **Plural** | -οι (*-oi*) | -οι (*-oi*) | -α (*-a*) |

Example: _ντόκιε_ (*ntókie*, 'local').

| Form | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter |
| ---- | --------- | -------- | ------ |
| **Nom. / Acc.** | ντόκιε (*ntókie*) | ντόκιε (*ntókie*) | ντόκιε (*ntókie*) |
| **Genitive** | ντόκιε (*ntókie*) | ντόκιε (*ntókie*) | ντόκιε (*ntókie*) |
| **Plural** | ντόκιοι (*ntókioi*) | ντόκιοι (*ntókioi*) | ντόκια (*ntókia*) |

The feminine **-ε** is identical to the masculine; a distinct feminine in **-α** (_ντόκια_) also occurs under Modern Greek influence.

#### Paradigm E6 — Ending in -ο, -ο, -ο

All three genders end in **-ο** (genitive **-ου**); as in E5, the feminine plural is **-οι**, identical to the masculine.

**Endings:**

| Form | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter |
| ---- | --------- | -------- | ------ |
| **Nom. / Acc.** | -ο (*-o*) | -ο (*-o*) | -ο (*-o*) |
| **Genitive** | -ου (*-ou*) | -ου (*-ou*) | -ου (*-ou*) |
| **Plural** | -οι (*-oi*) | -οι (*-oi*) | -α (*-a*) |

Example: _όμορφο_ (*ómorfo*, 'beautiful').

| Form | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter |
| ---- | --------- | -------- | ------ |
| **Nom. / Acc.** | όμορφο (*ómorfo*) | όμορφο (*ómorfo*) | όμορφο (*ómorfo*) |
| **Genitive** | όμορφου (*ómorfou*) | όμορφου (*ómorfou*) | όμορφου (*ómorfou*) |
| **Plural** | όμορφοι (*ómorfoi*) | όμορφοι (*ómorfoi*) | όμορφα (*ómorfa*) |

## Consonant mutations

At the point where a stem meets a suffix, certain consonants shift predictably. Two mutations are relevant for adjectives.

**ρ → ζ̌ in women's speech (before ι)**

In women's speech — a documented sociolinguistic feature of Tsakonian — *ρ* shifts to **ζ̌** when it comes directly before *ι*. This affects the masculine (*-ίου*) and feminine (*-ία*) forms of E4 adjectives, where the stem-final *ρ* immediately precedes the suffix-initial *ι*:

| Masculine (men's) | Masculine (women's) | Feminine (men's) | Feminine (women's) | Meaning |
|:------------------|:--------------------|:-----------------|:-------------------|:--------|
| βαρίου | βαζ̌ίου | βαρία | βαζ̌ία | heavy |
| μακρίου | μακζ̌ίου | μακρία | μακζ̌ία | long |

**τ → κ before -ι- (neuter)**

When the stem ends in *-τ* and the neuter suffix begins with *-ι-* (as in *-ιου*), the *τ* palatalises to **κ**:

| Masc. | Fem. | Neut. | Transliteration (neut.) | Translation |
|:------|:-----|:------|:------------------------|:------------|
| πρώτε | πρώτα | πρώ**κ**ιου | *prókiou* | first |
| τέταρτε | τέταρτε | τέταρ**κ**ιου | *tétarkiou* | fourth |

The same mutation appears in ordinal numerals and in several pronouns remodelled on the *-ιου* adjective pattern.

## Comparative and superlative

The comparative suffix **-ούτερε / -ουτέρα / -ούτερου** is added to the positive stem and declines like a *-ε / -α / -ου* adjective. Monolexical comparatives are more productive in Tsakonian than in standard Modern Greek.

| Positive | Comparative (M) | Comparative (F) | Comparative (N) | Meaning |
|:---------|:----------------|:----------------|:----------------|:--------|
| ἁτσ̌έ | ἁτσ̌ύτερε | ἁτσ̌υτέρα | ἁτσ̌ύτερου | big |
| μιτσί | μιτσούτερε | μιτσουτέρα | μιτσούτερου | small |

The monolexical **superlative** in *-τατε* (cited by Deffner) is rejected by Kostakis as non-Tsakonian; the superlative sense is instead expressed periphrastically (by context).
