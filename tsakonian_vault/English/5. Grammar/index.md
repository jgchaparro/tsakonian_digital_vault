This section covers Tsakonian grammar: how words are formed, inflected, and combined into sentences. It blends conservative Doric features inherited from Ancient Greek with unique innovations not found in Standard Modern Greek.

- [[5.1 Nouns]] — gender, number, and case, including the progressive loss of the genitive.
- [[5.2 Verbs]] — the aspect system (imperfective vs. perfective) and the periphrastic constructions that replace many Standard Modern Greek inflected forms.
- [[5.3 Adjectives]] — agreement with nouns in gender, number, and case.
- [[5.4 Pronouns]] — strong (emphatic) vs. weak (clitic) pronouns.
- [[5.5 Numbers]] — the Doric-derived numeral system, including gender agreement for 1–4.
- [[5.6 Adverbs]] — place, time, manner, and quantity modifiers, and their phonetic divergence from Standard Modern Greek.
- [[5.7 Prepositions and Postpositions]] — how prepositions largely take over the functions of the lost genitive case.
- [[5.8 Syntax]] — word order, agreement, clitic placement, and the predicative participle.
- [[5.9 Particles and Clitics]] — invariable grammatical particles and clitic placement rules, which vary between subdialects.
