> Learning Modern Greek is strongly advised, as almost all Tsakonian materials are in Greek and no translations to English are available.

## Learning Resources

For vocabulary lookups, use the [[8. Dictionary|Tsakonian Digital Dictionary]].

- [Για να κ̔οντούμε τα γρούσσα νάμου (*Gia na khondoúme ta groússa námou* — "To preserve our language")](https://tsakonianarchives.gr/gia-na-kontoyme-ta-groyssa-namoy-gia-na-kratisoyme-ti-glossa-mas-mia-gnorimia-me-ti-tsakoniki-24): Recommended first book. Bilingual Tsakonian–Greek only. Use screenshots of individual texts and ask any AI (Gemini, ChatGPT, etc.) to translate them into your language. All materials are of immense help.

- [Χρονικά των Τσακώνων – Τόμος Θ' (*Chroniká ton Tsakónon – Tómos Th'* — "Tsakonian Chronicles – Volume 9")](https://tsakonianarchives.gr/hronika-ton-tsakonon-tomos-th): After gaining some basic expertise with the above book, try reading and completing the exercises in this resource. Contains a series of stories in Tsakonian with a short vocabulary list translated to Modern Greek. If you do not speak Modern Greek, the same "screenshot and ask AI to tre
- anslate" approach applies.

- **Tsakonian sessions**: Prof. Maxim Kisilier imparts Tsakonian sessions via Zoom every Tuesday evening. To enroll, contact [Prof. Maxim Kisilier](https://www.facebook.com/share/1HqzZjJ5j1/) or the Tsakonian Archives at archivestsakonia@gmail.com.

- **Panos Marneris**: Contact Panos Marneris via Facebook, where he eagerly shares his experience with the Tsakonian language.
