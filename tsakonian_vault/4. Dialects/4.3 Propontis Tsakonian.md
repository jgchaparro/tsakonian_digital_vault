Propontis Tsakonian was spoken in the Sea of Marmara region (modern-day Turkey). This dialect is now extinct as its speakers were scattered throughout Greece after the 1922 population exchange between Greece and Turkey, and subsequently shifted to Standard Modern Greek [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].

## Towns and Villages

### Vatika / Mousatsa (Βάτικα / Μουσάτσα)

The main Tsakonian settlement in Propontis, located opposite Artaki on the southern coast of the Sea of Marmara, near the Aisēpos (Αἴσηπος) River. The village had over 150 Greek families in the early 20th century. Its economy was based on cereal crops and horticulture. The village name "Vatika" is believed to derive from **Vatika** (Βάτικα), the homeland region in southeastern Lakonia — a key piece of evidence for the settlers' Lakonian origins [[Kostakis Short Grammar of Tsakonian 1951.md|(Kostakis, 1951)]].

### Havutsi (Χαβουτσί)

A smaller settlement located roughly half an hour from Vatika [[Kostakis Short Grammar of Tsakonian 1951.md|(Kostakis, 1951)]].

## The 1922 Exodus and Refugee Settlement

The Propontis Tsakonians were displaced during the catastrophe of 1922. On 26 August 1922, the inhabitants of Vatika and Havutsi departed by steamship, arriving first at Pasalimani, then at Alexandroupoli, and finally at Aigio (23 October 1922). They were permanently settled in **Servia** (Σέρβια) near Kozani in 1924. The inhabitants of Havutsi were settled separately, in **Chionata** (Χιονάτα) and **Gkerleni** (Γκερλένι) [[Kostakis Short Grammar of Tsakonian 1951.md|(Kostakis, 1951)]].

### Dating the Colony

The date of the Propontis Tsakonian colony's founding is debated:

- **Koukoules** (1924): proposed a **13th-century** date, linking the colony to military settlers during the Nicaean Empire / Latin occupation period.
- **Amantos**: argued the colony could be **much later**, possibly even 18th century.
- **Pachymeres** provides testimony that Emperor Michael VIII Palaiologos resettled Lakones/Tsakones "συν γυναιξί και τέκνοις" (with wives and children) in Constantinople, supporting the possibility of Byzantine-era resettlement [[Kostakis Short Grammar of Tsakonian 1951.md|(Kostakis, 1951)]].
- **Kostakis** sided with a **later dating** (post-1668), reasoning that: (a) the dialect's excellent preservation is incompatible with a 13th-century migration, (b) Evliya Çelebi (1668) showed that Tsakonia still extended deep into Lakonia at that time with ανθηρά χωριά ("flourishing villages"), and (c) the village name "Vatika" points to Vatika in Lakonia as the settlers' origin, a region that contracted only after the Albanian raids and the Orlov events of the 18th century [[Kostakis Short Grammar of Tsakonian 1951.md|(Kostakis, 1951)]].

## Characteristics

- Reported to be more conservative than the Peloponnesian varieties [[What is the Tsakonian language Tsakonian Digital.md|(García Chaparro, 2026)]].
- Underwent significant influence from the Thracian Greek used by neighboring populations, bringing it closer to Standard Modern Greek [[What is the Tsakonian language Tsakonian Digital.md|(García Chaparro, 2026)]].

### Phonology: The "Open A" (α̈)

A unique feature of Propontis Tsakonian is the treatment of the unstressed vowel sequence **-ια** (/-ia/). This sequence frequently merges into a single "very open a" sound, represented in Kostakis' orthography by **α̈** (accented **α̈́**) [[Simple Kostakis Orthography Proposal.md|(García Chaparro, 2024)]]. This shift reflects a characteristic phonological reduction in the subdialect [[Kostakis Short Grammar of Tsakonian 1951.md|(Kostakis, 1951)]].

| Original (SMG/Ancient) | Propontis Form | Gloss             |
| ---------------------- | -------------- | ----------------- |
| _φτειάνα_ (φτιάχνω)    | **φτä́να**      | to make / prepare |
| _κρυάδα_               | **κρä́δα**      | coldness          |
| _μυαλό_                | **μäλέ**       | brain / mind      |

In other cases, the sequence results in the **ι** becoming a "very open e" sound (represented by **ة**) while the **α** remains distinct (e.g., _δράμια_ > _δράμةα_) [[Kostakis Short Grammar of Tsakonian 1951.md|(Kostakis, 1951)]].

### Clitic Placement (Medieval Greek Pattern)

Propontis Tsakonian preserves the medieval Greek pattern for clitic pronouns: if the verb is in initial position, clitics follow it (enclitic); otherwise they precede it (proclitic). This pattern also applies in other eastern dialects of Modern Greek [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]:

| Context          | Clitic Position | Example              | Gloss                 |
| ---------------- | --------------- | -------------------- | --------------------- |
| Verb-initial     | Enclitic        | _θωράκαΐ ν' τον αγό_ | 'They saw the rabbit' |
| Non-verb-initial | Proclitic       | _μα τ' παρακαλέσ'_   | 'I will ask you'      |

This contrasts with Peloponnesian Tsakonian, where clitics are proclitic in almost all environments.

### Future Particle _μα_

The Propontis subdialect forms the future with the particle _μα_ (as in _μα τ' παρακαλέσ'_ 'I will ask you'), which cannot be derived from the grammaticalization of the auxiliary _θέλω_ ('want') as in SMG and most Greek dialects. The origin of this particle remains an unresolved issue in Tsakonian linguistics [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].

### Periphrastic Subjunctive and Imperative

Propontis Tsakonian features periphrastic structures for the subjunctive and imperative, which are crosslinguistically rare for these moods (cf. Dahl 1985, Bybee et al. 1994: 104–124) and cannot easily be interpreted as the result of language decay [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].

## Documentation

- Kostakis provided significant documentation of this dialect by interviewing exiled speakers. His _Σύντοµη Γραµµατική της Τσακωνικής Διαλέκτου_ (1951) examines the northern subdialect and, for the first time, the Propontis subdialect [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].
- His principal informants for the Propontis material were: papa Thanasis Papadopoulos (Athens), N. Valsamoglou (Kokkinia/Piraeus), R. Marinaki and D. Drivanoglou (Nea Artaki, Euboea) [[Kostakis Short Grammar of Tsakonian 1951.md|(Kostakis, 1951)]].
- The dialect is covered in his three-volume _Dictionary of the Tsakonian Dialect_ (1986–87) [[What is the Tsakonian language Tsakonian Digital.md|(García Chaparro, 2026)]].
- Several unresolved issues remain due to the historical conditions that did not allow the collection of more and better quality material [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].
