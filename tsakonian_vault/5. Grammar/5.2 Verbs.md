# 5.2 Verbs

The Tsakonian verbal system is characterized by a mix of conservative Doric features and unique innovations. It maintains a system of aspects (Imperfective vs. Perfective) and voices (Active vs. Passive), often employing periphrastic constructions.

## Aspect and Voice

Aspect is a central feature of the Tsakonian verb, distinguishing between ongoing or repeated actions (Imperfective) and completed, single actions (Perfective).

### Active and Passive Voices

The passive voice is distinct from the active voice, with specific forms for different aspects and timeframes. Below is the paradigm for the verb _ορού_ (to see) [[Aspects of the Passive in Tsakonian.md|(Lysikatos, n.d.)]]:

| Aspect           | Time     | Active Voice | Passive Voice  |
| ---------------- | -------- | ------------ | -------------- |
| **Imperfective** | Non-Past | _ενί ορού_   | _ενί ορούμενε_ |
|                  | Past     | _εμά ορού_   | _εμά ορούμενε_ |
| **Perfective**   | Non-Past | _οραού_      | _οραθού_       |
|                  | Past     | _οράκα_      | _οράμα_        |

### Passive Participles

Passive participles in Tsakonian function primarily as **verbs** rather than adjectives, retaining verbal syntactic characteristics even in complex structures [[Aspects of the Passive in Tsakonian.md|(Lysikatos, n.d.)]]. This verbal nature is central to understanding the periphrastic system, since the participle in constructions like _ενί ορούμενε_ behaves syntactically as a predicate rather than a modifier.

- **Analytic Imperfective Participles:** Often end in _-μένε_ (e.g., _ορούμενε_).
- **Synthetic Perfective Passive Participles:** Retain verbal syntactic characteristics even when used in complex structures.

## Periphrastic Constructions

Tsakonian is the only Greek dialect with **exclusively compound (periphrastic)** present and imperfect forms [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]. The indicative is formed with the auxiliary verb 'to be' (_ενί/εμί_) + the participle of the main verb:

| Tense             | Form           | Gloss              |
| ----------------- | -------------- | ------------------ |
| Present           | _εμι ορού_     | 'I see'            |
| Imperfect         | _εμά ορού_     | 'I was seeing'     |
| Present passive   | _εμι ορούμενε_ | 'I am seen'        |
| Imperfect passive | _εμά ορούμενε_ | 'I was being seen' |

This distinguishes Tsakonian not only from all other Greek dialects but from most languages globally (cf. Haspelmath 1998) [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].

### Gender Distinction in Periphrastic Tenses

A remarkable feature of the Tsakonian periphrastic system is that the participle in the present and imperfect indicative agrees in gender with the subject. This means that these tenses morphologically distinguish whether the subject is masculine, feminine, or neuter — a feature unique among Greek varieties [[Kostakis Short Grammar of Tsakonian 1951.md|(Kostakis, 1951)]]:

| Gender    | Present ('I want') | Imperfect ('I wanted') |
| --------- | ------------------ | ---------------------- |
| Masculine | έμι θέλου          | έμα θέλου              |
| Feminine  | έμι θέλα           | έμα θέλα               |
| Neuter    | έμι θέλουντα       | έμα θέλουντα           |

For example, a man says _έμι ορού_ ('I see', masc. participle), but a woman says _έμι ορά_ ('I see', fem. participle).

### The Auxiliary έμι έχου ('I have')

Tsakonian expresses "to have" periphrastically as έμι έχου (literally "I am having"), corresponding to SMG έχω. This construction is built from the copula έμι plus the participle έχου, and is itself used as an auxiliary for compound tenses [[Kostakis Short Grammar of Tsakonian 1951.md|(Kostakis, 1951)]]:

| Tense          | Formation                   | Example                         |
| -------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Present        | έμι έχου                    | 'I have'                        |
| Imperfect      | έμα έχου                    | 'I had'                         |
| Perfect        | έμι έχου + perf. participle | έμι έχου φτατέ 'I have arrived' |
| Pluperfect     | έμα έχου + perf. participle | έμα έχου φτατέ 'I had arrived'  |
| Future perfect | θα έχου + perf. participle  | 'I will have arrived'           |

The full present paradigm of έμι έχου:

| Person | Singular | Plural    |
| ------ | -------- | --------- |
| 1st    | έμι έχου | έμε έχου  |
| 2nd    | έσι έχου | έτε έχου  |
| 3rd    | ένι έχου | είνι έχου |

### Laconian Origin of Periphrasis

Contrary to Aerts's (1965) assertion that these periphrases are an innovation, verbal periphrases leading to the modern Tsakonian system are attested in Hesychius's Laconian glosses [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]:

| Hesychius Laconian | Attic Equivalent | Meaning       |
| ------------------ | ---------------- | ------------- |
| ἐξηληµβώρ          | ἐξειληφώς (ἦν)   | 'to perceive' |

This proves that the periphrastic system is an **inherited Laconian feature** rather than an independent development within Tsakonian.

## Subjunctive vs. Indicative

Tsakonian is the only Modern Greek dialect that preserves **morphologically distinct endings** in the monolectic subjunctive, in contrast to the periphrastic indicative [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].

### Active Voice

| Mood            | Imperfective                     | Perfective                      |
| --------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| **Indicative**  | _εμι ορού_ 'I see'               | —                               |
| **Subjunctive** | _να ορίνου_ 'that I see (impf.)' | _να οράου_ 'that I see (perf.)' |

### Passive Voice

| Mood            | Imperfective                           | Perfective                           |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| **Indicative**  | _εμι ορούμενε_ 'I am seen'             | —                                    |
| **Subjunctive** | _να ορινούμα_ 'that I be seen (impf.)' | _να οραθού_ 'that I be seen (perf.)' |

The mediopassive present subjunctive paradigm shows a mixture of the person endings of the ancient subjunctive and optative. For example, _να γραφούμα_ 'I be written (pres. subj.)' is comparable with the ancient optative _γραφοίμην_, while the 3rd person singular _να γράφηται_ derives from the ancient subjunctive _γράφηται_ [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].

## Aspect Marking Morphology

Imperfectivity is marked by morphemes that follow the verb stem. The contrast between imperfective and perfective subjunctive forms illustrates this [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]:

| Imperfective     | Perfective | Gloss    |
| ---------------- | ---------- | -------- |
| _να απρού-κh-ου_ | _να απρού_ | 'spread' |
| _να ορ-ίν-ου_    | _να οράου_ | 'see'    |

The bolded segments _-κh-_ and _-ίν-_ are imperfective morphemes inserted between the stem and the ending.

### Transitivity Marking: The Suffix _-αΐχου_

The suffix _-αΐχου_ is widely used to derive transitive (causative) verbs from intransitive bases [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]:

| Intransitive | Gloss       | Transitive (_-αΐχου_) | Gloss                  |
| ------------ | ----------- | --------------------- | ---------------------- |
| _μοζού_      | 'feel pain' | _μοζαΐχου_            | 'cause pain'           |
| _γερού_      | 'get old'   | _γεραΐχου_            | 'cause someone to age' |

## Mediopassive Aorist

The mediopassive aorist paradigm in Tsakonian is particularly archaic [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].

### Person Endings

The ending _-μα_ in Tsakonian corresponds to _-μην_ of the middle aorist of athematic verbs in Attic:

| Tsakonian | Attic   | Gloss                        |
| --------- | ------- | ---------------------------- |
| _ορά-μα_  | ἐδό-μην | 'I was seen' / 'I was given' |

### Doric Perfect Preservation

The syncretism of the perfect and the aorist led to the preservation of aorists with labial or velar stems that correspond to **Doric perfects with voiced obstruents**, in contrast to Attic voiceless aspirates [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]:

| Tsakonian Aorist             | Doric (Messenian) Perfect              | Attic Perfect      |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| _εκρέβα_ [eˈkreva] 'I stole' | κεκλεβώς [kekleˈbοs] (IG 5(I).1390.75) | κέκλοφα [kéklopha] |
