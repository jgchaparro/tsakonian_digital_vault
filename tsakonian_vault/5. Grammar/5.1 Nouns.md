# 5.1 Nouns

Tsakonian nouns are classified by gender, number, and case, though the system has undergone significant simplification and reduction compared to its Ancient Greek roots. While many patterns align with Modern Greek, Tsakonian preserves distinctive archaic forms and unique developments, particularly in its declension classes and the progressive loss of the genitive case.

## Gender

Tsakonian has three genders: **masculine**, **feminine**, and **neuter**. Gender is marked by the definite article, the noun ending, and agreement with adjectives [[About the Genitive in Tsakonian Nouns.md|(Kisilier & Mertyris, 2018)]].

### Definite Article by Gender

| Case | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Nominative** | ο | α | το |
| **Genitive** | του | ταρ (vowels) / τα (cons.) | του |
| **Accusative** | τον | ταν (vowels) / τα (cons.) | το |

The feminine article variants result from **rhotacism** (intervocalic /s/ > /r/): *τας* > *ταρ* before vowels (e.g., *ταρ αγάκη* 'of love'). Before consonants, final /s/ and /n/ assimilated or were lost, leading to syncretism between the genitive and accusative articles (*τα*) [[About the Genitive in Tsakonian Nouns.md|(Kisilier & Mertyris, 2018)]].

## Case System

The Tsakonian case system primarily consists of three functional cases: **Nominative**, **Genitive**, and **Accusative**.

| Case | Primary Function | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Nominative** | Subject of the sentence | Fully functional |
| **Genitive** | Possession, relationship | Weakening; near-total loss in plural |
| **Accusative** | Direct object, object of prepositions | Fully functional; often replacing Genitive |

The **Dative** case has been entirely lost. Early records (e.g., Oikonomos, 1870) listed dative forms, but these were either accusatives with a preposition or errors influenced by Katharevousa [[About the Genitive in Tsakonian Nouns.md|(Kisilier & Mertyris, 2018)]].

### Rhotacism in Case Marking

The preservation of the ancient Doric/Laconian change of intervocalic /s/ to /r/ directly affects feminine case marking [[About the Genitive in Tsakonian Nouns.md|(Kisilier & Mertyris, 2018)]]:

- **Before vowels:** *τας* → *ταρ* (e.g., *ταρ αγάκη* 'of love'). This preserves a phonological distinction between genitive and accusative.
- **Before consonants:** The final /s/ of the genitive article and the final /n/ of the accusative article both assimilate or are lost, causing the cases to collapse into syncretism (e.g., *α νύ[ι]θη* → gen./acc. *τα νύ[ι]θη*).

## Plural Number

Tsakonian plural formation is largely uninflected for case in Peloponnesian dialects [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].

### Animacy Distinction

Masculine nouns show an animacy distinction in their plural suffixes [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]:

- **+animate:** Uses **-οι** (e.g., *αθρίποι* 'people', *υζοί* 'sons').
- **-animate:** Uses **-ου** (e.g., *τόπου* 'places', *ακ̒ού* 'vessels').

### Semi-Speaker Case Re-introduction

In the northern subdialect, semi-speakers sometimes restore **-ου** as an accusative plural marker (e.g., nom.pl. *οι ελάφοι* vs. acc.pl. *τιρ ελάφου*), reintroducing a case distinction into an otherwise syncretic paradigm [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].

## The Genitive Case and its Decline

The genitive case is undergoing progressive reduction and replacement. While functional in the singular for many nouns, it has nearly vanished in the plural [[About the Genitive in Tsakonian Nouns.md|(Kisilier & Mertyris, 2018)]].

### Decline in the Singular

- **Neuter nouns in -άτου and -ίου:** Modern speakers have almost entirely lost these forms (e.g., *σωμάτου*, *μαλίου* are rarely used) [[About the Genitive in Tsakonian Nouns.md|(Kisilier & Mertyris, 2018)]].
- **Feminine nouns in -έ:** Distinct forms like *κ̒αρέ* (fire) often coexist with uninflected variants (*κ̒άρα*) or have disappeared entirely [[About the Genitive in Tsakonian Nouns.md|(Kisilier & Mertyris, 2018)]].
- **Variation:** Some nouns show regularization toward the **-ου** pattern or outright loss of endings (e.g., *πέτσ̌ε* → gen. *πέτσ̌ε*).

### Loss in the Plural

The genitive plural is restricted to a few fixed expressions of age, quantity, or value [[About the Genitive in Tsakonian Nouns.md|(Kisilier & Mertyris, 2018)]].

| Nominative | Gen. Plural | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| αμέρα | αμερού | 'day' |
| γίδα | γιδού | 'goat' |
| δραχουμή | δραχουμού | 'drachma' |
| εριφό | εριφού | 'kid' |
| μήνα | μηνού | 'month' |
| χρόνε | χρονού | 'year' |
| ψαλία | ψαλιδού | 'scissors' |

Examples: *δέκα χρονού καμπζί* ('a ten-year-old child'), *δύ' μηνού καμπζί* ('a two-month-old child').

### Periphrastic Alternatives

Possession is increasingly expressed via two main strategies [[About the Genitive in Tsakonian Nouns.md|(Kisilier & Mertyris, 2018)]]:

1.  **Accusative Replacement:** *οι πούε τα κότ̒α* ('the feet of the hen').
2.  **Prepositional Phrases:**
    - **With *σε* ('to/in'):** *οι ψιλοί σ' έταν̇η τα γουναίκα* ('the eyes of this woman'). Coalescence occurs before the article: *οι ψιλοί τ̒ουρ αθρίποι* ('the eyes of the people').
    - **With *από* ('from'):** *οι πούε από τα καμπζία* ('the feet of the children').
