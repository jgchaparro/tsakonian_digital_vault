# General Description of the Tsakonian Language

Tsakonian (Greek: Τσακωνική διάλεκτος) is a highly divergent variety of Greek and the only modern variety that does not descend from the Hellenistic Koine. Instead, it derives directly from the Doric Greek dialect spoken in ancient Laconia (Sparta) [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]. It is spoken on the southeastern coast of the Peloponnese, in the Arcadia region of Greece.

## Classification and Uniqueness

Tsakonian occupies a unique place in Greek linguistics. It is classified as:

| Feature             | Description                                                                                              |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| **Language family** | Indo-European > Hellenic > Doric Greek                                                                   |
| **Origin**          | Laconian Doric (not the Hellenistic Koine)                                                               |
| **Status**          | Critically endangered [[What is the Tsakonian language Tsakonian Digital.md                              | (García Chaparro, 2026)]] |
| **Speakers**        | Estimated 1,000–2,000, mostly aged 50+ [[What is the Tsakonian language Tsakonian Digital.md             | (García Chaparro, 2026)]] |
| **Subdialects**     | Southern, Northern (both Peloponnesian), and Propontis (extinct) [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md | (Liosis, 2017)]]          |

No basic history of the Greek language is considered complete without an extensive discussion of Tsakonian. Major historical linguists — including Horrocks (2010), Browning (1983), Christidis (2014), and Adrados (2003) — all devote significant sections to the dialect [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]. Kontosopoulos (2001) places Tsakonian first in his survey of Modern Greek dialects, and Trudgill (2003) stresses its high degree of divergence from all other varieties [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].

## Key Linguistic Features

Tsakonian is distinguished by several features that set it apart from Standard Modern Greek (SMG) and all other Greek dialects.

### Phonology

- **Preservation of Doric α:** Where most Greek dialects shifted long /aː/ to /eː/ (and ultimately to /i/), Tsakonian keeps the original Doric /a/, e.g. _μαλί_ [mali] 'apple' < Doric μᾶλον (SMG μήλο) [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].
- **Retention of Digamma (ϝ):** The ancient phoneme digamma, lost early in most Greek dialects, survives in Tsakonian as /v/, e.g. _βαννέ_ [vane] 'lamb' < Cretan ϝαρήν [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].
- **Rhotacism of final /s/:** Final /s/ becomes /r/, e.g. _κακό(ρ)_ < AG κακός [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].
- **Additional phonemes:** Tsakonian has aspirated consonants (/kʰ/, /tʰ/, /pʰ/), postalveolar fricatives (/ʃ/, /ʒ/), and broader palatalization rules than any other Greek dialect [[Simple Kostakis Orthography Proposal.md|(García Chaparro, 2024)]].

### Verbal System

Tsakonian is the only Greek dialect with an exclusively periphrastic present and imperfect tense, formed with the auxiliary 'to be' + the participle: _εμι ορού_ 'I see' (lit. 'I am seeing') [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]. It also preserves morphologically distinct subjunctive endings, a feature lost in all other modern Greek varieties [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].

### Nominal System

The case system is reduced to three functional cases (nominative, genitive, accusative), with the genitive undergoing progressive loss, especially in the plural [[About the Genitive in Tsakonian Nouns.md|(Kisilier & Mertyris, 2018)]]. Masculine plural nouns show an animacy-based distinction between _-οι_ (+animate) and _-ου_ (−animate) [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].

### Syntax

Clitic pronoun placement differs between subdialects: Propontis Tsakonian follows medieval Greek rules (enclitic after initial verbs, proclitic elsewhere), while Peloponnesian Tsakonian has generalized proclisis [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]. Tsakonian also preserves the predicative participle after verbs of sensing or starting — unique among modern Greek dialects [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].

### Vocabulary

The Tsakonian vocabulary contains a rich Doric stratum, particularly visible in the basic vocabulary and in closed grammatical categories such as personal and demonstrative pronouns. Successive contact strata reflect interaction with various Koines (Hellenistic, medieval, modern) and other languages, especially Turkish and Romance languages. The Slavic element is minimal [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].

## Genetic Debate

The Laconian origin of Tsakonian was not always unanimously accepted. Scholars such as Pernot (1934), Brixhe (1996), and Nicholas (1998) proposed that Tsakonian arose as a variety of the Hellenistic Koine with Doric colouring. However, modern research — including evidence from Late Antique inscriptions and the Laconian glosses of Hesychius — confirms that numerous Tsakonian features are inherited directly from Laconian rather than being later innovations [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]].
