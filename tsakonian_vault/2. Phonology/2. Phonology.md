Tsakonian exhibits several unique phonological characteristics that distinguish it from Modern Greek, largely due to its Doric origins. **Phonology** is the study of the sound system of a language — which sounds exist, how they combine, and how they change in different environments.

> **IPA notation:** sounds throughout this page are given in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA), where each symbol represents a unique sound. Square brackets [ ] indicate actual pronunciation. For example, [r] = the rolled 'r' in Spanish, [θ] = the 'th' in *think*, [ʃ] = the 'sh' in *ship*, [ʒ] = the 's' in *measure*. For more on how these sounds are written in the Tsakonian alphabet and with diacritics, see [[3. Orthography]]. For the Doric historical origin of many of these features, see [[1.3 Doric heritage]].

## Archaic Features

- **Preservation of Doric α:** For example, [ˈmali] 'apple' < Doric μᾶλον (SMG μήλο). [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]
- **Preservation of Digamma (ϝ):** For example, [ˈvane] 'lamb' < Cretan ϝαρήν. [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]
- **Rhotacism of Final /s/:** *Rhotacism* is the change of /s/ into /r/. Final /s/ often becomes /r/. For example, [kaˈko(r)] < AG κακός. [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]
- **Vowel Reversal:** Tsakonian shows a reversal of the high back vowel /u/ development, which in Standard Modern Greek became /i/. [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]
- **Height Dissimilation of Vowel Sequences:** Vowel sequences undergo height dissimilation, e.g. [proˈδia] 'sheepskin', cf. Medieval Greek προβέα. [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]
- **Preservation of Ancient /eː/:** The ancient long /eː/ is preserved in Tsakonian, e.g. νέμα 'thread', χαμελέ 'low', ψελέ 'tall'. [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]
- **Reflexes of Ancient /ju/ (< υ /y/):** Ancient /ju/ appears differently depending on the preceding consonant:

| Preceding consonants              | Reflex | Examples                        |
| --------------------------------- | ------ | ------------------------------- |
| /k/, /γ/, /x/, /p/, /v/, /f/, /m/ | /ju/   | γκριούφου 'I hide'              |
| /t/, /ð/, /θ/, /l/, /r/, /n/      | /u/    | γουναίκα 'woman', π̒ουρί 'grain' |

- **Confirmation by Ancient Sources:** These archaic characteristics are confirmed by Late Antique inscriptions and the Laconian glosses of Hesychius, showing they are inherited elements rather than later innovations. [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]

## Unique Phonemes

A **phoneme** is the smallest unit of sound that can change the meaning of a word. Tsakonian features several phonemes that are not present in Standard Modern Greek:

- **Aspirated Consonants:** /kʰ/, /tʰ/, and /pʰ/ are consonants pronounced with a strong puff of air, as in English *kin*, *top*, *pit* (vs. *skin*, *stop*, *spit* which are unaspirated). These contrasts are absent in Modern Greek. (e.g., [kʰara] 'fire'). [[Simple Kostakis Orthography Proposal.md|(García Chaparro, 2024)]]
- **Postalveolar Fricatives:** /ʃ/ is the sound of English *sh* (as in *ship*) and /ʒ/ is the sound in French *jour* or the 's' in English *measure*. Both are absent from Standard Modern Greek (e.g., ['ʃina] 'mountain', ['kaʒi] 'walnut'). [[Simple Kostakis Orthography Proposal.md|(García Chaparro, 2024)]]
- **Voiceless Laminal Dental Affricate:** [t͡s̻] (e.g., [t͡s̻e] 'and'). [[Simple Kostakis Orthography Proposal.md|(García Chaparro, 2024)]]

## Unique Phonological Rules

Beyond its inventory of sounds, Tsakonian applies distinctive rules for how sounds interact and change in context:

- **Semi-vowel Metathesis:** *Metathesis* is the reordering of sounds within a word. A specific rule exists where the semi-vowel [i] is metathesized (moved) when preceded by a continuous dental consonant. For example, [a'θia] becomes ai'θa 'sister'. [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]
- **Secondary Aspirates:** For example, [aˈkho] < AG ἀσκός. The inherited nature of these aspirates is confirmed by Hesychius's Laconian glosses, e.g. Hesych. Lac. αἰκχούνα 'shame' = Attic αἰσχύνη (where κχ represents [kh]). Their presence in Late Antique inscriptions further shows they are inherited from Laconian rather than independent innovations. [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]
- **Final Vowel Change:** Final [-o] changes to [-e] after a coronal consonant. For example, [θile] 'friend' < AG φίλος. [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]
- **Consonant Palatalization:** *Palatalization* is the process where a consonant gains a 'y'-like quality when occurring before a front vowel (like [i]). Tsakonian imposes palatalization in many more environments than SMG and other Modern Greek dialects. Unlike most dialects, which palatalize only velars and sonorants /n/ and /l/, Tsakonian additionally palatalizes labials /p/ and /m/, and dentals /t/ and /r/. Dialect-internal differences exist in the application of these rules. [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]

- **Propontis Vowel Merge (α̈/α̈́):** In the Propontis dialect, the sequence /-ia/ frequently merges into a single "very open a" sound (e.g., _φτä́να_ 'to make', _κρä́δα_ 'coldness'). This sound is represented in Kostakis' orthography with an umlaut (α̈). [[Kostakis Short Grammar of Tsakonian 1951.md|(Kostakis, 1951)]]

| Target consonant | Example    | SMG equivalent |
| ---------------- | ---------- | -------------- |
| /p/ (labial)     | [aγˈaci]   | αγάπη          |
| /m/ (labial)     | [ˈɲiγdale] | αμύγδαλο       |
| /t/ (dental)     | [ciˈmu]    | τιμώ           |
| /r/ (dental)     | [ˈʒizi]    | ρύζι           |

*Dialect decay* refers to the gradual erosion of distinctive dialect features as a language community shifts towards using a dominant variety (here, Standard Modern Greek). It differs from simple language death in that rather than features just disappearing, they are often replaced by mixed or hybrid forms. See [[4.4 Dialect Comparison]] and [[4.2 Northern Tsakonian]] for specific examples of decay patterns.

| Phonological variable                  | Description       |
| -------------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| Aspirated vs. unaspirated consonants   | e.g. /kʰ/ vs. /k/ |
| Palatal vs. palate-alveolar consonants | e.g. /c/ vs. /tʃ/ |

Phonological contrasts associated with regional identity are more resilient to language decay — that is, they take on an increased "social load" or become "emblematic" — and are retained longer than contrasts without such associations. [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]

Semi-speakers of the dialect produce hybrid forms that combine Tsakonian-specific rules with SMG phonological rules. A characteristic example involves metathesis and palatalization:

| Stage                | Form             | Rule applied                                                                        |
| -------------------- | ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Tsakonian base       | [aˈθia] 'sister' | —                                                                                   |
| Tsakonian metathesis | [aiˈθa]          | Semi-vowel [i] metathesized before continuous dental                                |
| Semi-speaker hybrid  | [aiˈθça]         | Additional application of SMG palatalization (cf. SMG [vaˈθia] → [vaˈθça] 'deeply') |

This complication of the phonological system shows that dialect decay differs from language death in general: rather than simple reduction, dialect characteristics are adapted to the parameters of the dominant variety (SMG), and the common linguistic base is extended by the parallel or combined use of forms from two closely related varieties. [[Tsakonian Studies State of the Art.md|(Liosis, 2017)]]

## Phonology in Word Formation

### Systematic Vowel Changes

Kostakis documented systematic vowel shifts from Ancient/Koine Greek to Tsakonian. These long-term sound changes are called **sound correspondences** and are one of the main tools historical linguists use to prove that Tsakonian descends from ancient Laconian rather than from the Koine [[Kostakis Short Grammar of Tsakonian 1951.md|(Kostakis, 1951)]]. Refer to [[1.3 Doric heritage]] for the historical context.

| Change | Example            | Gloss                           |
| ------ | ------------------ | ------------------------------- |
| ε→ι    | πιτεινέ < πετεινός | 'rooster'                       |
| ε→ο    | κορασί < κεράσι    | 'cherry'                        |
| η→ε    | αμέρα < ημέρα      | 'day'                           |
| ο→ε    | ερέ < ωρός         | 'beautiful' (also reflects ω→ο) |
| ω→ου   | ούρα < ώρα         | 'hour'                          |

Additional vowel phenomena include:

- **Aphairesis** (loss of an initial vowel): κεί < εκεί 'there'
- **Syncope** (loss of a vowel in the middle of a word): πρεσού < περισσού 'much'
- **Prothesis** (addition of a vowel at the start): αστάρι < στάρι 'wheat'
- **Epenthesis** (insertion of a vowel inside a word): κοράτση < κράτση 'handhold'

### Consonant Cluster Transformations

Tsakonian is notable for extensive transformations of Greek consonant clusters. Kostakis catalogued over 50 such changes [[Kostakis Short Grammar of Tsakonian 1951.md|(Kostakis, 1951)]]:

| Cluster | Becomes                 | Example        | Gloss      |
| ------- | ----------------------- | -------------- | ---------- |
| τρ→ʒ    | ʒ (voiced postalveolar) | ʒία < τρία     | 'three'    |
| σκ→κχ   | κχ (aspirated velar)    | ακχού < ασκός  | 'wineskin' |
| κτ→χτ   | χτ                      | νύχτα < νύκτα  | 'night'    |
| πτ→φτ   | φτ                      | φτερέ < πτερόν | 'feather'  |

### Metathesis

**Metathesis** (the reordering of sounds within a word) is a productive process in Tsakonian [[Kostakis Short Grammar of Tsakonian 1951.md|(Kostakis, 1951)]]:

- κορβάτσι < κοβράτσι 'small basket'
- φουρνί < φρουνί 'eyebrow'

Specific phonological rules apply during the process of word composition:

- **Connecting Vowel Omission (Synalepha):** The standard connecting vowel /-o-/ is frequently omitted when the second component of a compound begins with a vowel.
  - Example: _μπουχαντάρα_ [buxa'ndara] (where -ο- is dropped before -α-) [[Composition in the Tsakonian Dialect.md|(Galanou, n.d.)]].
- **Hiatus Preservation:** In certain instances, the connecting vowel is retained despite creating a hiatus between two vowels.
  - Example: _μηλοαχρά_ [miloa'xra] (apple-pear) [[Composition in the Tsakonian Dialect.md|(Galanou, n.d.)]].
